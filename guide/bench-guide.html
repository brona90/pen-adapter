01

The tool

One instrument does every reading here: a 150 mm digital caliper with 0.01 mm resolution, a metric button, and outside jaws that thin to a knife edge at the tips — you will be sitting those tips in thread grooves. Nothing else is needed; a pitch gauge is nice but this guide works without one.

02

Ground rules

  1. Zero every time. Wipe the jaws, close them, press ZERO. Open and close: it must read 0.00. Then press mm.
  2. Touch, don't squeeze. Plastic compresses; a firm squeeze costs 0.1 mm — the whole print clearance. Let the jaws land.
  3. Three reads, rotated. Every measurement three times, turning the part about a third of a turn between reads. Type all three; the sheet keeps the largest diameter (jaws slipping into a groove read small, never large) and the median length.
  4. Square to the axis. If the number wobbles as you rock the tool, you're tilted. The true value is the minimum of the rock, then the maximum across rotations.
  5. Slipping tips? Press the thread firmly into Blu-Tack or a bar of soap and measure the imprint. It works surprisingly well for pitch.
03

Bottle side

Unscrew the grey knurled insert from the Vac 20A. Everything in this section is measured on the insert or on the bare neck. The adapter's male thread is a copy of the insert's, so the insert is the reference part.

A1diameter

Major diameter of the insert thread BOTTLE_THREAD_MAJOR_DIA

Outside jaw tips across the thread crests, jaws square to the axis. Three reads, rotating.

BOTTLE_THREAD_MAJOR_DIA—largest of three
A2diameter

Minor diameter sanity check

Push the jaw tips down into opposite grooves until they seat on the roots. Not a parameter — it tells us the tooth depth. A 60° V thread is about 0.54 × pitch deep; much shallower means a truncated form and the male side wants +0.05 mm clearance.

tooth depth / pitch—needs A1 and A3
A3pitch

Pitch, without a pitch gauge BOTTLE_THREAD_PITCH

  • Lay the insert on the bench. One jaw tip on the crest of the first full thread, the other on the crest of the last one you can reach, jaws parallel to the axis.
  • Count the crests between the tips, inclusive. Pitch = span ÷ (crests − 1). Spanning five crests makes a 0.05 mm caliper error worth 0.01 mm on the pitch.
  • Do it again from the other end. The two should agree within 0.03 mm; the sheet rounds to the nearest 0.05 because real pitches are 0.75, 0.8, 1.0, 1.25, 1.5.
BOTTLE_THREAD_PITCH—rounded to 0.05
A4starts

Number of starts BOTTLE_THREAD_STARTS

Look at the tip of the thread: how many separate ridges begin at the end face? Almost certainly one. To be sure, screw the insert in, pencil-mark insert and neck, back it out exactly one turn, and measure how far it rose with the depth rod. Starts = rise ÷ pitch.

BOTTLE_THREAD_STARTS—
A5length

Thread length BOTTLE_THREAD_LENGTH

Depth rod from the underside of the insert's flange to the tip. The parameter is 0.5 mm less than measured, so the flange O-ring seats before the thread bottoms in the neck.

BOTTLE_THREAD_LENGTH—median − 0.5
A6hand

Hand HAND

Screwing the insert into the bottle: clockwise tightens = right-hand. Both threads share this setting; the pen cap is checked the same way in B4.

HAND— 
A7seal

Neck rim BOTTLE_RIM_SEAL_DIA

Insert out. Inside jaws in the neck opening for the rim ID; outside jaws across the neck, right at the top face, for the rim OD. The O-ring lands on the flat ring between them, so that land must be wider than the 1.35 mm groove with margin — the sheet checks it.

BOTTLE_RIM_SEAL_DIA—mean of ID and OD
A8context

Two numbers about the bottle

The insert's flange OD is how big the hex may grow (17 mm was a guess). And with the bottle about half full, the depth rod from the neck rim down to the ink surface — that feeds the reach check in 05.

hex may be up to—across corners ≤ flange OD
04

Pen side

Unscrew the Asvine's section from the barrel so you hold it nib-out. The adapter's female thread copies the thread the cap screws onto, and its top O-ring is pressed by the shoulder the cap normally seats against.

B1diameter

Major diameter of the section thread PEN_THREAD_MAJOR_DIA

Jaw tips across the crests, square to the axis. Three reads, rotating, largest wins.

PEN_THREAD_MAJOR_DIA—largest of three
B2diameter

Minor diameter sanity check

Tips into the grooves, as A2. Tooth-depth check against the pitch from B3.

tooth depth / pitch—needs B1 and B3
B3pitch

Pitch PEN_THREAD_PITCH

Same crest-count method as A3. Pen threads are short — you may only get three or four crests, so do it from both ends, and use the soap imprint if the tips slip.

PEN_THREAD_PITCH—rounded to 0.05
B4starts

Starts — do not skip this PEN_THREAD_STARTS

Pen cap threads are very often 2- or 3-start. Cap on, pencil-mark cap and section, turn exactly one turn, measure the axial travel with the depth rod. Starts = travel ÷ pitch. Two or three switches the profile to "trap" in the parameter block automatically. While the cap is on: clockwise tightens confirms right-hand.

PEN_THREAD_STARTS—
B5length

Threaded band PEN_THREAD_LENGTH

From the shoulder (the step the cap seats against) to the last crest. Enter as measured; the script adds 1 mm of unthreaded relief below it so the section's last thread never meets the cone before the shoulder meets the O-ring.

PEN_THREAD_LENGTH—median
B6seal

Shoulder land PEN_SHOULDER_SEAL_DIA

Outside jaws across the shoulder OD, then the diameter where the thread ends against the shoulder (close to B1). The flat ring between them squashes the top O-ring — confirm it is flat, not a curve or a chamfer.

PEN_SHOULDER_SEAL_DIA—mean of the two
B7bore

Nib and feed BORE_DIA

The widest point of nib and feed together — usually across the nib shoulders, sometimes the feed fins. Largest of three, plus 1.0 mm.

BORE_DIA—largest + 1.0
B8reach

Shoulder to nib tip

Depth rod from the shoulder to the tip of the nib. The original insert sits inside the neck; this adapter lifts the pen by the hex and the tube, so the nib must still reach the ink. Section 05 does the arithmetic.

nib below ink surface—needs A8 rim → ink

Getting it made

  
Three routes. Threads at a 1 mm pitch are the deciding factor: a 0.4 mm nozzle draws them, but only just; resin and nylon sintering draw them cleanly. Gauges are cheap in any process — spend the precision on the final part.

Assemble and test

      
    
        
  • Seat both rings. Screw the adapter into the neck finger-tight — the hex is for grip, not torque.
    • 
        
  • Screw the section in until the shoulder just compresses the top ring; a quarter turn more, no further.
    • 
        
  • Water first. Half-fill the bottle with water, invert over the sink for a minute. Any weep at either face means that O-ring, not the threads.
    • 
        
  • Vacuum test with water: pull the plunger. Water rising = sealed. Bubbles at a face and no rise = that seal, or the nib isn't submerged (B8).
    • 
        
  • Then ink. Rinse the adapter after each fill; dried ink in the female thread is the first thing that will make it feel tight.
